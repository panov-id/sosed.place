# sosed — Privacy Policy

**Last updated: 16 September 2026**

## 1. Who we are (controller)

sosed (the "**Service**", at **sosed.place** and its apps) is operated by **Evgenii Panov**, acting as a private individual under the brands **PSYTICAN & PEJEDED**, Limassol, Cyprus, the **data controller**.

Contact for privacy: **support@sosed.place**

## 2. Our approach

sosed is **ephemeral, area-based, and low-identity by design**. We process the **minimum data** needed to run it, and most content is **deleted automatically** as it fades. We do not build advertising profiles and we do not sell your data.

## 3. What we process

- **Device identity.** An identifier and a key pair created in your browser, so the Service can work without a traditional account. **We do not fingerprint your browser.** The identity is tied to this browser: clearing site data or a private window creates a new one. Your identity lives on one device at a time. You can move it to another yourself by typing a nine-character code shown on the first, and the first one freezes when you do — there is no code to scan and no second device running alongside. If you clear the data, the identity comes back only with the paper recovery code you were shown once when you created it; without that code neither we nor you can bring it back, and the conversations do not come back either way.
- **Session and keys.** With your identity our node keeps **one live session**: its public key, a device label such as "Chrome, Android" as your browser sends it, and when the session was created and last seen. So that the history on your device can be unlocked, it also keeps **a share of the key** to that history (32 random bytes, meaningless on their own), a hash derived from your PIN and a count of wrong PIN attempts, and a hash of half of your paper recovery code with your long-lived key wrapped under the other half. We know neither your PIN nor the code and cannot reset either.
- **Area.** An approximate area worked out from your time zone, IP address and browser language, or a point you place by hand. Two settings are yours: how far you look, and how precisely you are seen. **We never ask your browser for your exact coordinates — there is no button that does, and the permission is never requested.** Your point is placed from those indirect signals and moved by hand on a diagram; a permission nobody can grant is a permission that cannot leak.
- **Feed languages.** Up to three languages you chose for the feed, kept with your identity because the feed is filtered on our server; by default taken from your browser.
- **Age.** The age you enter, as a number of years, and your age-filter preference, to keep the space appropriate.
- **Name.** The display name you set — required, shown to the people you match with, and checked by our automated moderation at registration and at every change (§6a).
- **Appearance.** The theme (light, dark or as in your system), the contrast step and the accent colour you chose in the app. We keep them with your identity on our node, separately for each of our services you use it through, so that they come with the identity when you move it or bring it back with the paper recovery code. Nobody else sees them, and they are used for nothing but drawing the app for you.
- **Content.** The messages, likes (plus/skip), and chats you create — most of which fades and is deleted automatically. With a message we keep the language it was detected to be written in, worked out on our own server and used only for the language filter. With your identity we keep your matches, the people you blocked, and your reports against messages until they are dealt with.
- **Hidden and blocked.** The phrases you hid for yourself, kept until the phrase fades, and the people you blocked, with the date of the block, so that you can lift it.
- **Tables.** If you sit at a table: the lines you say there (they are public and go through the same check as the feed), your applications to play, your seat and the score.
- **What you accepted.** For each of the Terms, this Policy and the Community Guidelines: the date of the edition you accepted, a digest of its text and when you ticked the box.
- **Waitlist email.** If you request access: your email address, the source of the request, which face you came through, and the language, colour and light-or-dark setting you were using — those three so the email we send you looks like the site you asked from — and whether you asked for early access.
- **Support.** The text of your request, the email address if you gave one, the request number and our answer, kept for a year (§5).
- **Technical data.** Basic logs (e.g. IP, timestamp, user agent) needed for security and to run the Service.
- **Counters about your identity.** Totals — how many likes you gave and received, how many matches and chats you have had. Numbers only: no text, no names, nobody you spoke to. Alongside them, **the moments of your publications and refusals within the last hour** (only the latest four and six are kept: an older one is cut when a new one is written, and anything older than an hour is ignored) and **the date of your first publication**, to the day — for the posting limit, the 15-minute pause and whether your report about a link in an offer counts, which needs the date itself because it is compared with the day each offer came out. Kept while the identity lives.
- **Content-security-policy reports.** When your browser blocks something our page tried to load, it posts a report: the page, what was blocked, which rule stopped it, your browser's user agent and the time. No identifier and no address; kept 30 days. It tells us our own page is misconfigured.
- **Following a link in an offer.** A business offer may carry a link, and it is the only place in the Service where one exists. The link goes through **our own redirect** rather than straight out, so that we can switch it off if the destination turns bad. Passing through it we increment **one number: how many times that offer's link was followed.** We do not record who followed it, when, from which address, or with which browser, and the number is shown to the business as a count and nothing else. Saved offers are not counted at all: they sit on your device and nothing about them reaches us.
- **Error logs.** When something breaks, we record a technical error report (error message, page URL, your browser's user agent, and non-identifying context) so we can diagnose and fix it. Used only for reliability, not profiling.
- **If you publish offers for a business.** The owner's email address — the only way into the business cabinet is a link sent to it — and the contact person's name; for each venue, its name, its postal address (confirmed by an envelope sent there) and whether it is verified; the complaints about its offers and the business's replies.
- **A complaint about an offer.** Its text and date, and — for a complaint that a discount was refused or misled — **your email address, which is required**, because it is the only way to tell you the decision. A complaint about a link carries no email.
- **A report of illegal content.** If you report content as illegal, we keep your reasons, your name and email address (we ask for neither where the report concerns the sexual abuse of children), and **a copy of the reported content taken at that moment** — because the content itself disappears on its timer, and without the copy we could neither examine the report nor justify what we did. We keep this to comply with a legal obligation, not because you consented, and it is used for nothing else.

## 4. Why we process it (legal bases)

The waitlist now rests on **steps taken at your request before a contract** — you leave an address asking to be called, and that is the request; ask us and the entry still goes. This covers pre-publication moderation with the moments of your publications and refusals, server logs and error reports, content-security-policy reports, the nightly backups, the admin audit log, our own page counter, and examining complaints about offers.
- **Legal obligation** (Art. 6(1)(c)): reports of illegal content and the copy taken with them, answering requests to exercise your rights, informing the authorities under Art. 18 DSA, and wherever else we must retain or disclose data by law.

## 5. Retention and ephemerality

- **A feed message** lives 4 hours 20 minutes and is then **deleted, not hidden**. What remains is a counter of how many there were, without the text and without the author.
- **A chat's contents** live on your device for the shorter of the two times the two of you chose. On our servers they are **kept only until delivered** — if you are away when one arrives, it is held for you, encrypted, until you come back, until the conversation ends or until your session is frozen, whichever comes first, and we cannot read it at any moment of that. **At most 200 messages are held for you in one conversation:** when more arrive, the oldest are dropped without notice to either of you. **What our node does keep is the outline of a conversation**: that one exists between two identities, when it last moved, the span each of you chose, and when it ended. Not a word of what was said, and no copy of it. **One thing more, since 2026-09-09: if you play a game inside a chat, the node keeps the state of that game** — the position on the board, whose turn it is, the players' hands, the undrawn stock, **the word set in hangman** and the score of the pair. It is not encrypted, because the node judges the play by the rules and a judge without the position cannot judge; the hangman word also goes through moderation (§6a). Apart from that word, no reply of yours is in there; the state is tied to the conversation, and it is deleted the moment the conversation is.
- **Profile data** (identity, age as a number of years, name, settings other than appearance, the counters and what you accepted) is kept while the identity lives. **An identity lives until it is closed:** you close it by starting over, and **an identity with no live session for a year is closed** as well. **A closed identity is deleted 30 days after closing** — the thirty days are not a way back, there is none, but time to carry out a report or a decision tied to it; once it is closed, the paper recovery code no longer brings it back.
- **A session** is kept while it lives; one not seen for a year is removed together with its key share. **Matches** live as long as the conversation they opened, **a block** while it holds, and **a report against a message** until it is dealt with. **At a table**, what was said and the board go when the table does, on its silence span.
- **Appearance** (§3): while the identity lives; it is deleted at once when you close the identity.
- **The "stepped away" label** is kept on each of your live conversations while you are away, and goes when you write or make a move in that conversation, or when the conversation ends.
- **The moments of your publications and refusals, and the date of your first publication** (§3): while the identity lives.
- **A waitlist email** is kept until launch and for one year after, then deleted — sooner if you ask.
- **A report of illegal content**, the copy taken with it, and the explanation sent to the author: **1 year**, then deleted — an anonymous count is all that remains. **A business profile and the complaints about its offers**: kept while the business publishes, and deleted a year after its last offer. If the business was suspended for systematic justified complaints, a keyed hash of the venue's address and the date of suspension are kept one more year, so that waiting out the year does not lift the suspension. A suspension the venue asked for itself ("this is not us") leaves nothing behind.
- **A support message**: 1 year. When you start over, its link to your identity is cut at once; the text, and the email if you gave one, are still kept for the year as a record of how it was handled; a report of illegal content sent through support is moved to the notice register and deleted from support. **Detailed page views**: 14 days. **The admin audit log**: 1 year. **A nightly backup of our database**: 14 days, so anything you delete leaves our backups within a fortnight. **Server logs and client errors**: 30 days.
- **Personal data is not written to logs** — not an email address, not the text of a message, not an identifier.

## 6. Location

We match by **area**, not by precise location, and we never display your exact position or your distance to anyone else. The area is approximate by default and is marked as such.

Your message is tied to a **zone around you rather than to a point** — you choose how large that zone is — so that a handful of messages cannot lead anyone to an address. Your browser is never asked for precise coordinates: the point comes from your time zone, address and language, and you move it by hand.

## 6a. Moderation

Every message — and your display name, at registration and at every change — is checked before it is published, **on our own servers**: the text is not sent to anyone. Explicit content is refused for everyone: there is no setting that turns it on, so there is nothing to switch and nothing to circumvent.

**We do not classify what a message is about, and we never infer which groups you belong to.** No feed is filtered on such a basis. The result of a check is not stored beside the message.

**Chats are not checked.** What you write inside a chat is not checked and is not read by us — it travels from your device to the other person's, encrypted, and we cannot open it. **The word set in hangman is the exception on our side:** it is part of the game's state (§5), our server sees it, and it goes through the same check as a feed message before the other player sees it; a refused word has to be replaced, and the refusal counts towards the 15-minute pause. The other exception comes from the other side: if the person you are talking to reports a message as illegal, they describe it in their own words and quote what they choose — their device attaches nothing, and we have nothing to attach. What they wrote is kept for a year with the report. Moderation governs what is published to the feed, where strangers see it; two people talking is not publication.

If a message is not published, you are told, and told why. These decisions are automatic and final: a message can be edited and sent again, and a pause ends by itself after 15 minutes (Terms §11).

## 7. Sharing and processors

We do not sell your data. We share it only with service providers ("processors") that help us run the Service, under appropriate agreements, for example:

- **Server hosting** (Hetzner, Germany) — the server our node and our database run on. The database is ours and no other party uses it, but it sits on Hetzner's machine, so Hetzner processes everything kept in it on our behalf.
- **Site delivery and storage** (Bunny) — serving the site, the edge log of its CDN, and object storage for the waitlist and the nightly backups of our database.
- **Email delivery** (Resend) — every letter we send: the answer to a waitlist request, replies to support messages, letters about a report of illegal content to the person who sent it and to the author, letters to a business about its venues, offers and complaints, the sign-in link to the business cabinet, the decision on a complaint about a discount, and the invitations the admin panel sends. Resend may process data outside the EU; that transfer rests on the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses and Resend's certification under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework.
- ~~Analytics (Google Analytics 4)~~ — **removed 2026-09-10**. Visits are counted by our own counter, which sends nothing anywhere.

**Donations are not in this list.** If you follow the donation link in our footer, you leave the Service. PayPal then handles your payment under its own terms as an **independent controller**, not as our processor: we do not send it your data, we receive no payment details, and we cannot tell who donated. Donating is never required to use the Service.

We may disclose data if required by law or to protect safety and rights. **Where we suspect a criminal offence threatening someone's life or safety, we inform the law enforcement or judicial authorities concerned, or Europol**, with the information the report holds (Art. 18 DSA; legal basis — Art. 6(1)(c) GDPR).

## 8. International transfers

Processors may operate outside your country, so here is exactly where things stand rather than a general assurance. **Our server, database and storage sit in Germany, with no replication elsewhere** — the server and database at Hetzner, the storage at Bunny in its German region. **Everything the Service keeps about you is there:** your identity with its session, key share and hashes, your age, name and appearance, the counters about your identity, what you accepted, the feed, your matches, blocks and reports, the tables, the outline of your conversations and the state of games played in them, the offers, business accounts and complaints, reports of illegal content with their copies, support messages, the admin audit log, server logs and error reports, the page counter, the waitlist, and the nightly backups. A chat's contents are not kept there beyond delivery: they pass through encrypted, and what waits for you while you are away waits encrypted (§5). What can cross a border is the edge log our CDN keeps (an IP address and the address of the page) and the waitlist emails held in that CDN's object storage. **Our contract with that provider does not include the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses**, and its terms permit processing worldwide; we have accepted that as a residual risk rather than papered over it, and we will say so here if it changes. Email delivery through Resend may leave the EU under the Standard Contractual Clauses and the EU-US Data Privacy Framework (§7). A donation is not a transfer by us: you leave the Service for PayPal, which acts under its own terms as an independent controller.

## 9. Your rights (GDPR)

You have the rights to **access, rectify, erase, restrict, object to, and port** your data. Nothing we process rests on your consent (§4), so there is no consent to withdraw. Because the Service is ephemeral, much data is deleted automatically. To exercise your rights, contact **support@sosed.place**. You may also complain to your local data-protection authority (in Cyprus, the Office of the Commissioner for Personal Data Protection).

## 10. Children

The Service is for users **13+**, and we do not verify age — we ask, and we take the answer. Saying otherwise would be a claim we cannot back: any check strong enough to be worth something would mean collecting documents from everybody, which is a worse trade than the one it fixes.

This section used to say that anybody under 18 needs a parent's consent. We have removed that sentence rather than kept it as decoration: nobody was asked for such a consent, nothing recorded it, and a promise nobody keeps is worse than an absence stated plainly. **What we do instead is build the product so that a 13-year-old is not thrown in with adults:** anyone in the adult pool (21 and over) is never shown anyone under 19, and everyone under 19 is cut off from 21+ entirely, nothing is ever targeted at anybody by profile, and nothing is stored to build one from. If you are a parent and something here concerns you, write to **support@sosed.place** — a person reads it.

## 11. Security

We take reasonable technical and organizational measures to protect data. No method is perfectly secure; keep your device safe, since your identity lives on it.

## 12. Local storage / cookies

We use browser **local storage** for your device identity and preferences (name, age, language, and a copy of your appearance — theme, contrast and accent — whose original is kept with your identity, §3; and which of the in-app hints you have closed). **Also on your device, and nowhere else:** receipts for the reports of illegal content you sent, so that you can see the decision — starting over does not erase them, they have their own erase button; the offers you saved, until you erase them; and, in the browser's database (IndexedDB), your conversation history, the draft of an unsent phrase and a refused phrase with its reason, encrypted with a key assembled from your PIN and the share our node keeps (§3). We do not use advertising cookies and we do not sell or share data with ad networks.

**Analytics on the website — removed on 2026-09-10.** This used to describe Google Analytics 4, off by default and loaded only after you pressed "accept" in a cookie banner. There is no banner any more and no analytics script: visits are counted by our own counter on our own server, which sets no cookie, sends nothing to anybody, and keeps detail for 14 days before reducing it to a number. Nothing here now rests on your consent, which is also why nothing here asks a 13-year-old for a parent's.

**Our own page counter.** We count page views on our own servers, and there is nothing in it to consent to. Per view we record the page path, the page language, the **host** of the referring site (never its full address, which can carry your search terms), a coarse screen size (mobile / tablet / desktop) and whether this was the first view in this browser tab. We do **not** record your IP address, your user agent, or any identifier — nothing is stored on your device beyond a flag that disappears when you close the tab, so the counter cannot recognise you on a later visit or across sites. It exists to tell us how many people read which page in which language. Legal basis is our **legitimate interest** in knowing whether the site works.

Inside the Service, once you have an identity, you confirm a new edition of this Policy, the Terms or the Community Guidelines with one checkbox before you publish or open a chat; nothing is recorded as accepted until you tick it. The "last updated" date at the top is the current edition.
